The Absolute Beginner's Guide to Linux

Got your recommendation? Here is how to test it risk-free on your computer without erasing your Windows files or affecting your current setup.

STEP 1

Download the ISO File

An ISO file is an exact digital clone of an installation DVD or drive. When you visit the official website of your recommended distro (e.g. Linux Mint, Ubuntu, Fedora), click on the 64-bit ISO download button.

💡 Pro Tip: For most users, choose the default desktop edition (e.g. Linux Mint Cinnamon, Ubuntu Desktop LTS, or Fedora Workstation). The file is typically 2GB to 4GB in size.
STEP 2

Create a Bootable USB Flash Drive

You will need a clean USB flash drive (at least 8 GB). You cannot simply drag-and-drop the ISO file into the flash drive; you must "flash" it using a boot creation tool.

Ventoy (Recommended)

Install Ventoy once to your USB. After that, you can simply drag & drop multiple ISO files straight onto the USB!

Download Ventoy →
Rufus (Windows)

The most popular standalone tool on Windows. Select your USB drive, choose the ISO, leave partition scheme as GPT/UEFI, and click Start.

Download Rufus →
STEP 3

Boot from Your USB Flash Drive

Insert your prepared USB drive into your PC. Turn the PC completely off, then turn it on and immediately tap your brand's Boot Menu key continuously until the menu appears:

DELL / LENOVO
F12
HP
F9 or Esc
ASUS / ACER
F8 or F12
CUSTOM DESKTOP
F11 or Del

Select your USB Drive (often labeled UEFI: USB Flash Drive) and press Enter.

STEP 4

The "Live Environment" – Test Risk Free!

One of Linux's greatest superpowers is the Live Session. The OS runs entirely from your RAM and USB. Nothing is written to your hard drive yet.

  • Connect to your Wi-Fi network and verify internet connectivity.
  • Test audio playback via YouTube or a video.
  • Check keyboard brightness keys, touchpad gestures, and sleep mode.
  • If you don't like it, simply unplug the USB and reboot — your Windows or Mac is 100% untouched.
STEP 5

Dual Booting vs Clean Installation

When you click the desktop shortcut "Install Linux", the graphical wizard gives you two safe choices:

Option A: Install Alongside Windows

Shrinks your Windows partition safely. Every time you turn on your PC, a friendly menu (GRUB) lets you pick between Windows and Linux.

Option B: Erase Disk & Install Linux

Best for dedicated or revived secondary laptops. Gives 100% of the drive to Linux for clean performance. (Back up personal files first!)

BONUS

Essential Linux Terminal Cheat Sheet for Newcomers

While modern distros like Mint and Zorin require zero terminal usage, having these basic commands handy makes troubleshooting effortless:

# 1. Update package lists and upgrade apps (Ubuntu, Mint, Debian, Pop!_OS)
sudo apt update && sudo apt upgrade -y

# 2. Check disk space available on your drives
df -h

# 3. Check memory (RAM) usage in real time
free -m

# 4. View directory contents
ls -la

# 5. Display system and kernel specs
uname -r
